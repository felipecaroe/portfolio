Designing for
Imperfect Intelligence.

What should people see and be able to do when AI is slow, uncertain or wrong?

View the slides

The talk

I spoke at Diseño e IA by Arcasiles about the shift from interfaces that follow commands to AI products that respond to what people intend. The same request can produce different results. The product still needs to show what is happening, explain uncertainty and give people a way to recover when something fails.

I shared three ways to design for those moments before launch. A pre-mortem helps a team imagine failures it might miss. A failure-mode table turns those possibilities into decisions about what people see and what they can do. Norman’s gulfs of execution and evaluation help explain why asking in plain language can be easy while judging the result remains difficult.

Raúl Marín Calleja spoke about design judgement as AI speeds up the work. María Rogles López followed with a live design-system audit of the Arcasiles site. See the event programme.
