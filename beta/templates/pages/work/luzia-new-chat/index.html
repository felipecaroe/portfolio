02 / Release scope

A deliberate reset.

I focused the first release on conversation, while preserving access to essential tools and previous threads.

01

Open directly into a new chat.

Login leads into chat. A short welcome and nearby suggestions clarify the first action; the drawer holds earlier threads.

02

Keep a focused set of tools.

Text, voice and add stay in one compact composer. Visual tools and response settings have their own destinations.

03

Reduce the number of competing destinations.

Fewer entry points and character choices keep the opening clear. The wider prototype keeps other capabilities reachable.

04

Define what belongs in the release.

Voice-first date-of-birth onboarding belongs to the separate prototype exploration, outside the initial release.

03 / Experience principles

Quiet structure.
Useful cues.

Fewer choices, with essential actions still easy to find.

01

Welcome, then make room.

Use one concise invitation. Keep returning threads lighter so the greeting does not interrupt someone who already knows what they need.

02

Let the composer carry the next action.

Text, voice and add stay in a compact row. The later prototype uses the composer gradient to communicate idle, listening and generating states without replacing the whole control.

03

Let prompts give way.

Suggestions stay near the composer until typing or the keyboard needs the space.

04

Keep tools within reach.

The drawer holds thread history and visual tools. A short portrait-led picker makes character choice recognisable.

04 / Selected states

Designed as a flow.

Three prototype states show the invitation, keyboard and thread history. The wider interaction covers both themes and visual tools.

05 / System and states

One language.
More than a screen.

I connected the screen design with reusable components, semantic colour and transitions that explain changes of state.

A

One surface across light and dark.

The interactive mockup moves through light and dark modes and shows how the welcome, composer, drawer and visual tools respond across the flow.

B

Motion communicates state.

The welcome builds in sequence; the drawer pushes the chat surface; visual tools arrive from blur, then snap between cards. The composer gradient calms as the conversation takes over.

C

Bind colour to its role.

I mapped colour to semantic roles shared by the design and interactive prototype. Light and dark use the same component structure, with roles that change appearance across modes.

D

Carry the detail through device states.

Keyboard space, prompt visibility and composer height are part of the interaction specification. The small-screen baseline keeps the conversation usable when the device has less room.

06 / Scope boundaries

A focused release.
A broader exploration.

The initial experiment shipped a smaller chat-first experience. The prototypes explore the wider interaction model, including onboarding that was excluded from that release.

Initial experiment

Core experience

Chat-first entry, a compact composer, reduced destinations and a smaller character choice.

Designed in Figma

Onboarding and variants

Login, date of birth by voice or keypad, confirmation and handover, explored in light and dark. These onboarding steps were excluded from the first release.

Out of scope

Features considered

Chat search, routines, favourites and wider character discovery were considered beyond the first experiment.

Design decision01

Help someone start. Then get out of the way.

Suggested actions sit beside the composer until the person starts typing or opens the keyboard. I made that help temporary: it demonstrates what Luzia can do, then releases the space to the conversation. The keyboard state is part of the design, especially on smaller devices.
