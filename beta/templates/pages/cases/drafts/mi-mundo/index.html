Inside the work

Speak freely. See the idea take shape.

The prototype makes its reasoning visible: listen to the idea, reflect an interpretation, offer a visual direction, then reveal something the person can share.

The challenge

An empty prompt asks for too much too early.

A person may know the feeling they want without knowing a template, layout or product term. I explored a voice-first start that accepts an unstructured description. The first job is to give them something recognisable to react to before asking for more precise choices.

My approach

Let people correct the interpretation before the build.

The assistant reflects spoken input as organised groups that can be corrected in one tap. A visual direction follows, then the generated world and publishing action. I prototyped the interaction in both an expressive visual language and Luzia’s design system, allowing the flow to be reviewed separately from its styling.

Design decision

Make creation orderly and the result expressive.

The private creation steps keep one decision clear at a time. The public-facing reveal can be dense, playful and personal. I kept those two jobs distinct, retaining a fixed underlying structure and shared icons in the prototype. The prototype explores how those two visual behaviours can coexist in one product.
